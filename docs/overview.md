# Overview

Digital identity systems typically answer a narrow set of questions:

• Who is this user?
• What attributes can they prove?
• What permissions have they been granted?

They rarely answer deeper questions about authority and capacity:

• In what capacity is this actor operating?
• By what authority may they bind themselves or others?
• What scope does that authority have?
• When does it expire?
• How can it be revoked?

As a result, many systems collapse complex human relationships of mandate, delegation, and consent into simplified models of account ownership or key control.

SILT Core addresses this gap by defining a semantic layer that formalises:

• status
• standing
• authority
• consent
• revocation

These primitives allow systems to model not just identity, but the conditions under which action is legitimate and binding.

The project is specification-first. It defines normative language and structural primitives rather than prescribing specific implementations.

SILT Core is designed to be technology-agnostic and compatible with multiple environments including civic technology, governance infrastructure, digital public systems, and decentralised networks.
