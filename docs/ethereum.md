# Why This Matters for Ethereum

Ethereum introduced programmable coordination through smart contracts.

Decentralised organisations, treasuries, and governance systems can now operate without central platforms.

Yet most Ethereum systems still rely on simplified identity assumptions.

A wallet address signs a transaction. A multisig controls funds. A token represents voting power.

These mechanisms execute rules but often fail to model the deeper semantics of authority.

A signature alone does not indicate:

• the capacity in which an actor is operating
• whether authority is delegated or original
• the scope of that authority
• when authority expires
• how authority may be revoked

SILT Core addresses this by defining a semantic layer that makes authority explicit.

By formalising capacity, mandate, consent, and revocation, SILT enables digital systems to model how an actor is authorised to act, not merely whether a key signed a transaction.

Ethereum provides programmable execution.  
SILT provides the authority grammar that makes execution legitimate.
