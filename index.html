SILT Core

Semantic infrastructure for plural encounters.

SILT Core v0.2 — semantic architecture finalised

SILT Core provides a semantic architecture through which participants, collectives and systems operating under different legal, cultural, customary, private, institutional or technical orders may become mutually legible at moments of encounter without requiring those orders to collapse into a single ontology or surrender their own sources of authority.

SILT is concerned with encounter rather than assimilation.

Source → Standing → Presentation → evaluation at the encounter

The v0.2 semantic architecture has completed its pre-freeze stress tests and is now frozen. Release packaging, repository migration and publication are underway.

The encounter

A Participant arrives from somewhere. SILT does not reduce that “somewhere” to an issuer, registry or credential authority. It is represented through Source.

From Source arises or is grounded a relational position: Standing. Standing is relational and Source-grounded. It is not a universal credential, a platform status, or a self-certified badge.

For a particular encounter, the Participant need not reveal the whole relationship. Instead, the Participant makes a bounded, present-tense projection: Presentation.

Evaluation then occurs at the encounter. The receiving Participant, order or system may evaluate what has been presented without becoming the source of the Standing it encounters.

Profile Expression

An encounter may also receive one or more Profile Expressions: bounded, selective and non-exhaustive expressions of the semantic conditions relevant to evaluation.

A Profile Expression may arise from a legal, customary, cultural, relational, contractual, institutional, governance or other normative context. It does not represent or exhaust that context. The originating substrate remains prior to, independent of, and unencumbered by the expression.

Presentation and Profile Expression are therefore distinct. Presentation is what a Participant brings into the encounter. Profile Expression states encounter-relevant conditions under which that Presentation may be evaluated.

Multiple Profile Expressions may coexist or conflict. SILT does not silently merge, rank or universalise them.

What v0.2 evaluates

Evaluation may concern Standing and, where relevant, Evidence, Authority, Consent, Reliance, Action, Attribution, Revocation and Obligation.

For each evaluated semantic condition, SILT uses three outcomes: SATISFIED, NOT_SATISFIED, or INDETERMINATE.

INDETERMINATE is a legitimate result. SILT does not manufacture an answer by importing an unstated legal, institutional, cultural or normative rule. Where a condition cannot be adequately expressed without material distortion, it need not be forced into evaluation.

SILT also does not infer a single aggregate result unless the relevant Profile Expression supplies a composition rule.

Where SILT sits

Semantically thick. Operationally thin.

SILT standardises the boundary at which relational legitimacy becomes selectively legible across systems. It does not attempt to standardise the originating ontology or normative order itself.

Authentication, credential formats, key management, transport, registries, runtime policy, dynamic authorisation, technical capability systems and execution machinery remain below or adjacent to the SILT semantic layer.

DID/VC systems may carry identifiers and attestations. Capability systems may express technical permissions. Wallets may sign. Policy engines may decide. Agents and smart contracts may execute. SILT preserves the semantic thread that explains what those actions mean in context.

Semantic hand-off, not semantic surrender.

Why this matters

Digital systems increasingly connect actors who do not share one source of law, authority, identity or legitimacy. That includes Indigenous and customary orders, private agreements, institutions, commercial systems, DAOs, AI agents and technical protocols.

The hard problem is not simply identifying everyone or granting more permissions. It is preserving enough meaning at the boundary for an encounter to proceed without requiring one order to absorb the other.

SILT makes that boundary explicit: where Standing comes from, what is Presented, which conditions apply, what Authority exists, what may be relied upon, what obligations persist, and what happens when authority or consent is revoked.

AI and delegated systems

AI agents may act through recursive delegation chains, but technical capability does not itself establish Standing or Authority.

SILT does not attempt to make AI systems legal persons. It provides a semantic grammar through which authority provenance, scope, reliance, attribution, obligation and revocation can remain legible as actions move across human, institutional and machine systems.

An agent with a wallet is not the same thing as an agent with Standing.

What SILT Core is not

  • Not a universal identity model
  • Not a universal legal ontology
  • Not a universal authorisation engine
  • Not a credential or DID/VC replacement
  • Not a conflict-of-laws system or universal verifier
  • Not a law engine or universal adjudicator
  • Not a universal method for defining personhood, collective representation, Binding or liability

Status

  • v0.1 remains the currently tagged public release
  • v0.2 semantic architecture is finalised and frozen
  • Source → Standing → Presentation → evaluation at the encounter is the settled architectural seam
  • Profile Expression is the bounded encounter-relevant expression construct
  • Release packaging and migration of public specification materials are underway

Open infrastructure

SILT Core is developed as open semantic infrastructure. The Core is intended to remain implementation-neutral so that different legal, cultural, institutional and technical systems can adopt or test the grammar without being forced into one platform or execution stack.

Concrete bindings, carriers, validators and downstream implementations may evolve separately. They should not quietly redefine the semantic Core.
